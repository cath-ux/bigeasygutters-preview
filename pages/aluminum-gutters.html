Aluminum gutters

What are aluminum gutters and where do they fit best?

Aluminum gutters are lightweight metal channels that resist corrosion and need little upkeep, so they suit many homes and businesses around New Orleans. They are made in K-style and half-round shapes, come in a range of colors and suit owners who want a dependable system without a high-maintenance material.

Weighing aluminum for your roofline? Call us or contact us to book a free estimate and look over color samples.

Why aluminum

What makes aluminum a practical gutter metal?

Aluminum balances weight, corrosion resistance and upkeep. It generates its own protective oxide coating, it is light to carry and hang, and it asks for little beyond keeping the trough clear, so the system keeps doing its job without constant attention.

  1. 1

    A protective oxide coating

    The Aluminum Association notes that aluminum naturally generates a protective oxide coating that resists corrosion, so the metal defends its own surface.

  2. 2

    Light on the roof edge

    There is less weight to carry along the fascia than with a heavier metal, which keeps handling and hanging simple.

  3. 3

    Colors to match

    We offer a variety of gutter colors and materials, so the finish can blend with your siding, trim and roof.

  4. 4

    Simple routine care

    The City of New Orleans historic-district roofing guide advises clearing gutters regularly, typically every spring and fall.

Sources: Aluminum Association, Aluminum in Green Buildings and the City of New Orleans HDLC, Guidelines for Roofing (May 2024), section 05-12.

Side by side

How does aluminum compare with the other gutter materials?

Every gutter metal trades one strength for another. Aluminum is light and corrosion resistant with modest upkeep, copper is prized for its look, steel for strength, vinyl for a paint-free plastic channel and zinc for a protective surface that forms on its own. Here is the short version.

MaterialKnown forWorth weighing
AluminumLightweight, corrosion resistant, low upkeepA softer metal that can dent, and it should not touch copper
CopperA warm color that weathers to a blue-green patinaThe most expensive material, and it needs compatible fasteners
SteelToughness in rough weather, with plenty of colors and stylesIts protective coating needs watching if it gets scratched
VinylLight, rust-proof plastic that needs no paintingA plastic channel, so strength and finish differ from metal
ZincA protective patina that forms as it weathersCosts more than aluminum, the most affordable material

Treat this as a starting point. The right pick for your roof gets settled at your free estimate.

Straight answers

What do people get wrong about aluminum gutters?

Three points cause most of the confusion: whether aluminum rusts, how tough it is and what colors it comes in. Here is how each one holds up against what trade and city sources actually say.

Myth

Aluminum gutters rust like iron ones.

Fact

Aluminum contains no iron, so it does not rust. It forms a protective oxide coating instead. It can still corrode where it touches certain other metals, copper above all, so the two should be kept apart.

Myth

Aluminum is too soft to make a serious gutter.

Fact

It can dent, and the City of New Orleans roofing guide lists dents and punctures among its weak spots, which is fair. Light weight is the other side of that trade, and many homes are well served by it.

Myth

Aluminum only comes in plain white.

Fact

Looking after it

How do you look after aluminum gutters?

Aluminum care is simple: keep water moving, watch for sagging and keep the metal apart from copper. Four habits cover it, and we can take care of the cleaning for you.

  1. 01

    Clear debris twice a year

    The City of New Orleans guide advises cleaning gutters regularly, typically every spring and fall, before leaves and roof grit hold moisture against the metal.

  2. 02

    Watch the flow in rain

    Water that stands in the trough or spills over the lip points to a clog or a sag that deserves a look.

  3. 03

    Fix sagging early

    A sagging run holds water, and standing water can lead to mold or pests, so a drooping section is worth having checked.

  4. 04

    Keep copper clear

    If copper trim or flashing sits near your gutters, make sure the two never touch and that copper runoff does not drain onto the aluminum.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Are aluminum gutters a good choice for New Orleans homes?

They are a practical one for many homes. Aluminum is lightweight, resists corrosion through its own oxide coating and needs little upkeep, and it comes in colors that blend with your exterior. Whether it is the best fit depends on your roof, your budget and the look you want, which we review at your free estimate.

Do aluminum gutters rust?

No, aluminum contains no iron, so it does not rust the way steel can. The Aluminum Association notes that it naturally generates a protective oxide coating that resists corrosion. The exception is contact with other metals, especially copper, which can set off galvanic corrosion, so the two should never touch or share runoff.

How long do aluminum gutters last?

It depends on the quality of the material, how the run is installed and how well it is cleaned and looked after, so we do not promise a number of years. Debris left to sit and sagging runs wear out any gutter sooner. A free estimate lets us look at what you have now.

Can aluminum gutters be color matched to my home?

Yes. We offer a variety of gutter colors and materials, and a project specialist brings color samples to your free estimate. That lets you hold the samples up to your exterior before you decide, and the finish you pick is yours to settle.

Can aluminum gutters sit next to copper trim?

They can share a house, but not direct contact. The Copper Development Association names aluminum as a principal metal of concern beside copper and advises keeping copper runoff from draining onto bare aluminum. Keep that in mind for trim and flashing, and mention any copper on your house at your free estimate.

How do aluminum gutters compare with vinyl?

Both are lightweight and need little upkeep. Aluminum is metal, with the strength and finish choices that come with it, while vinyl is a plastic that never needs painting. The city's HDLC roofing guidelines caution that vinyl may fracture once temperatures drop. Which suits you depends on look, budget and style.
